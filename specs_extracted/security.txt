Eco Saathi 2.0 – Security and Privacy Plan
Version 1.0 · Draft · 15 October 2026 · Companion to the PRD, Technology Stack and System Architecture
This plan sets the security and privacy requirements for the new store. It is a technical and planning document, not legal advice. The compliance points in section 9 should be confirmed with a qualified lawyer before launch.
1. Security Goals
Protect customers' personal and payment information.
Keep the store available and its orders and prices accurate.
Stop attackers from abusing the website, admin panel, chatbot or messaging channels.
Collect only the data needed, and keep it only as long as needed.
Detect problems early and recover quickly.

4. Application Security Controls
4.1 Authentication and Sessions
Customers log in with phone or email OTP; OTPs are short-lived, single-use and rate-limited.
Staff accounts require strong passwords plus multi-factor authentication, and use individual accounts only (no shared logins).
Passwords, where used, are hashed with a modern algorithm such as Argon2id or bcrypt.
Sessions use secure, HttpOnly, SameSite cookies, expire after inactivity and are revoked on logout or password change.

4.3 Input and Output Handling
Validate all inputs on the server (type, length, format), including file uploads (allowed types and sizes, malware scan, stored outside the web root).
Encode output and sanitise any rich text from the blog editor.
Send security headers: Content-Security-Policy, Strict-Transport-Security, X-Content-Type-Options, Referrer-Policy and frame protection.
4.4 API Security
Public APIs are rate-limited; admin APIs are not reachable from the open internet except through authenticated sessions.
Webhooks from payment, shipping and WhatsApp providers are accepted only with valid signatures and from expected sources, and are processed idempotently.
Error messages shown to users never reveal system details.
5. Payment Security
Card, UPI and net-banking details are entered on the payment gateway's hosted page or secure fields, so they never touch our servers. This keeps the store's PCI DSS scope small; the exact questionnaire level should be confirmed with the gateway.
Only the gateway reference, status and amount are stored.
The order total is recalculated on the server at payment time; client-side values are never trusted.
A scheduled job compares paid orders against the gateway's records to catch missed or fake confirmations.
Refunds require an authorised role and are logged.

Customers should see a short notice that they are chatting with an AI assistant, with a link to the privacy policy and an easy way to reach a human.
7. WhatsApp and Email Security
Use only the official WhatsApp Business Platform with approved message templates; never use unofficial automation tools.
Store the access tokens in the secrets manager and rotate them on a schedule or after staff changes.
Send marketing only to customers who have opted in, and honour opt-outs immediately.
For email, set up SPF, DKIM and DMARC for the domain to prevent spoofing and improve deliverability.
Never ask customers for OTPs, passwords or card details over chat or email, and say this in the templates.
Links in messages point only to the official domain.

10. Monitoring and Incident Response
10.1 Monitoring
Alerts for repeated failed logins, unusual order or refund patterns, large data exports, new admin users, webhook signature failures and error spikes.
Weekly review of security alerts and dependency vulnerabilities.
10.2 Incident Response Steps
Detect and confirm: an alert or report is verified and an incident lead is named.
Contain: disable compromised accounts, rotate keys and secrets, block malicious traffic, and take affected features offline if needed.
Assess: find what data and which customers are affected, and how long it was exposed.
Notify: report to CERT-In and other authorities where required, and inform affected customers and the board or owner as the law and the privacy policy require.
Recover: restore from clean backups, patch the cause, and monitor closely.
Learn: write a short post-incident review with actions and owners.
Keep an emergency contact list (owner, developer, hosting provider, payment gateway, WhatsApp provider, legal adviser) in a secure place.
